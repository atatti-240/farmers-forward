WHAT TO DO  (most important first)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Reduce salinity (leach + drain)  [SEVERE]
     Leach salts with good-quality water once drainage is adequate (apply ~6
     in extra water to push salts below the root zone).
     Rate  : Leaching fraction sized to soil; add drainage if water ponds.
     Cost  : ~$20 to $60/acre
     When  : Pre-season, when you can move water through the profile.
     Note  : Test your IRRIGATION water too -- Brazos water in the Fort
     Bend/Richmond reach runs saltier downstream, so over-irrigating with it
     adds salt. Meanwhile grow salt-tolerant cotton or sorghum.
     Affects: 4 of 4 zones

  2. Lower pH / manage alkalinity  [MODERATE]
     Apply elemental sulfur and use acidifying fertilizers (ammonium sulfate).
     On calcareous Blackland clay, fully lowering pH is slow and costly --
     lean on tolerant crops + chelated micronutrients instead.
     Rate  : 562-762 lb/acre elemental sulfur
     Cost  : ~$169 to $309/acre (sulfur ~$0.30-0.55/lb)
     When  : Apply and incorporate several months ahead; oxidation to acid takes weeks-months and warm soil.
     Note  : Test for free lime first. If the soil fizzes with acid it is
     calcareous and sulfur demand is very high -- prefer cotton, sorghum and
     other alkaline-tolerant crops.
     Affects: 4 of 4 zones

  3. Correct nitrogen deficiency  [MODERATE]
     Apply nitrogen fertilizer (urea 46-0-0 or UAN). Split the application on
     corn/leafy crops to limit leaching.
     Rate  : 142-162 lb N/acre (~309 lb urea)
     Cost  : ~$78 to $106/acre (N ~$0.55-0.75/lb)
     When  : At/near planting; split-apply for corn (pre-plant + side-dress).
     Note  : Rate sized to a generic ~40 ppm soil-N target; specify a crop for
     a tailored rate. A legume cover crop can fix 40-100 lb N/acre
     biologically and cut this bill over time.
     Affects: 2 of 4 zones

  4. Correct phosphorus deficiency  [MILD]
     Band phosphate (DAP 18-46-0 or triple superphosphate) near the seed row
     for best early uptake.
     Rate  : 63-78 lb P2O5/acre
     Cost  : ~$38 to $57/acre (P2O5 ~$0.60-0.90/lb)
     When  : At planting (banded/starter).
     Note  : Banding beats broadcasting on high-pH soils where P gets tied up.
     Affects: 2 of 4 zones

  5. Stop over-applying phosphorus  [MILD]
     Halt all P fertilizer and manure. Protect water: buffer strips, cover
     crops, and erosion control to stop P-laden runoff.
     Rate  : 0 lb P2O5/acre until soil test P drops below 150 ppm.
     Cost  : $0 (a saving) + buffer/cover-crop cost
     When  : Immediately, and ongoing.
     Note  : High P is a runoff / water-quality liability, not a yield gain.
     Draw it down through crop removal over several seasons.
     Affects: 1 of 4 zones
